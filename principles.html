Opening principles

1. Fight for the center

The four central squares (d4, d5, e4, e5) are the high ground: pieces placed there control the most squares. Start with 1. e4 or 1. d4, and answer with a central pawn or a piece that presses the center (e5, d5, c5, Nf6).

2. Develop your minor pieces quickly

Knights and bishops first, usually knights before bishops (Nf3 and Nc3 almost always belong there; bishops have more options). Every move in the opening should bring a new piece into play. Don't move the same piece twice without a good reason.

3. Castle early

A king in the center is a target once files open up. Castle within the first 6–8 moves in most games. That also connects your rooks, which ends the opening phase.

4. Don't bring the queen out early

An early queen gets chased around by enemy knights and bishops — each attack on her is a free developing move for your opponent. Scholar's mate threats (Qh5) only work against beginners and leave White worse when defended correctly.

5. Make few pawn moves

Two or three pawn moves are enough to free your pieces. Every extra pawn move is a move not spent developing, and pawns can never move backwards. Especially avoid f3/f6 and g4/g5 early: they expose your king.

6. Watch what your opponent threatens

Before every move ask: what did that last move attack? Most beginner games are decided by a piece left hanging, not by deep strategy. Check captures and checks first.

Where to start

Pick one opening as White and one defense against each of 1. e4 and 1. d4 as Black, and play them in every game. Recommended first: the Italian Game or London System as White; the Caro-Kann or Scandinavian against 1. e4; the Queen's Gambit Declined or Slav against 1. d4. They are marked with ★ in the openings list.
